    Worksheet 4.1.4 Similar Triangles and the Pythagorean Theorem
    

    An extremely important concept in physics, trigonometry, calculus, and even carpentry is the notion of similar triangles. We will briefly discuss how to recognize similar triangles and what properties they have before returning to the ideas we explored in Worksheet 4.1.1.
    🔗
    Combining the definitions of sine and cosine with similar triangles and the Pythagorean theorem gives us tools that make these functions extremely useful in a variety of situations.
    🔗

    Definition 16. Similar Triangles.

    When two triangles have exactly the same three angles, we say that they are similar triangles or that the triangles are similar.
    🔗
    🔗
    The triangles shown in Figure 17 are similar because angle \(C\) is the same as angle \(D\) (\(\angle C\cong \angle D\)), angle \(B\) is the same as angle \(E\) (\(\angle B\cong \angle E\)), and angle \(A\) is the same as angle \(F\) (\(\angle A\cong \angle F\)).
    🔗
    We say that \(\angle C\) and \(\angle D\text{,}\) \(\angle B\) and \(\angle E\text{,}\) \(\angle A\) and \(\angle F\) are corresponding angles, and similarly, we say that sides \(\overline{CB}\) and \(\overline{DE}\text{,}\) sides \(\overline{BA}\) and \(\overline{EF}\text{,}\) sides \(\overline{AC}\) and \(\overline{FD}\) are corresponding sides.
    🔗
    Furthermore, we say that \(\triangle CBA \sim \triangle DEF\text{,}\) and the order matters!
    🔗

    Theorem 18. Corresponding Sides of Similar Triangles.

    Given two triangles, \(\triangle MNE\) and \(\triangle PQR\text{,}\) when the triangles are similar \((\triangle MNE\sim\triangle PQR)\text{,}\) the lengths of the corresponding sides are proportional.
    🔗
    🔗
    Applying Theorem 18 to Figure 17, for example, we have that
    \begin{equation*} \frac{f}{a}=\frac{e}{b}=\frac{d}{c}, \end{equation*}
    and, equivalently,
    \begin{align*} \frac{a}{c}&=\frac{f}{d},& \frac{a}{b}&=\frac{f}{e},& \frac{b}{c}&=\frac{d}{e}. \end{align*}
    🔗

    Theorem 19. The Pythagorean Theorem.

    For any positive numbers, \(a\text{,}\) \(b\text{,}\) and \(c\text{,}\) if \(a\) and \(b\) are the lengths of two legs of a right triangle and \(c\) is the length of the hypoteneuse, then \(a^{2}+b^{2}=c^{2}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Furthermore, for any triangle with side lengths \(a\text{,}\) \(b\text{,}\) and \(c\text{,}\) if \(a^{2}+b^{2}=c^{2}\text{,}\) then the triangle is a right triangle, the sides with lengths \(a\) and \(b\) are the legs, and the side with length \(c\) is the hypotenuse.
    🔗
    🔗

    1.

    In Figure 20, \(\overline{OA}\) has length \(0.5\)m, \(\overline{OB}\) has length \(1\)m, and \(\overline{OD}\) has length \(3\)m.
    🔗

    (a)

    Explain why \(\triangle{}OBA\) is similar to \(\triangle{}ODC\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Both triangles share \(\angle{}AOB\cong{}\angle{}COD\text{,}\) and \(\angle{}DCO\) and \(\angle{}BAO\) are both right angles. Because the measures of the three angles of any triangle add to \(180^{\circ}=\pi\) radians, we must have \(\angle{}OBA\cong\angle{}ODC\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Since all three angles of \(\triangle{}OBA\) are congruent to the respective angles of \(\triangle{}ODC\text{,}\) the two triangles are similar, \(\triangle{}OBA\sim{}\triangle{}ODC\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Use the fact that \(\triangle{}OBA\sim{}\triangle{}ODC\) to find the length of \(\overline{OC}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(1.5\)m.
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    We will represent the length of a segment as the two endpoints, so, for example, we will say that \(OA=0.5\)m.
    🔗
    We know that \(OA=0.5\)m, \(OB=1\)m, and \(OD=3\)m.
    🔗
    Because \(\triangle{}OBA\sim{}\triangle{}ODC\text{,}\) we know that \(\frac{OA}{OB}=\frac{OC}{OD}\text{,}\) so substituting the known lengths, we have
    \begin{align*} \frac{OC}{OD}=\frac{OA}{OB}&\rightarrow\frac{OC}{3\text{m}}=\frac{0.5\text{m}}{1\text{m}}\\ &\rightarrow{OC}={3\text{m}}\frac{0.5\text{m}}{1\text{m}}\\ &\rightarrow{OC}={1.5\text{m}} \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    Use the Pythagorean theorem to find \(AB\text{,}\) the length of \(\overline{AB}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\text{m}\approx 0.866025\)m.
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    From the Pythagorean theorem, since \(\triangle{}BAO\) is a right triangle, we have \(\left(BA\right)^{2}+\left(OA\right)^{2}=\left(OB\right)^{2}\text{,}\) so \(\left(BA\right)^{2}+\left(0.5\right)^{2}=\left(1\right)^2\text{,}\) so
    \begin{align*} \left(BA\right)^{2}&=\left(1\right)^2-\left(0.5\right)^{2}\\ &=1-0.25\\ &=0.75\\ &=\frac{3}{4} \end{align*}
    so
    \begin{align*} BA&=\sqrt{\frac{3}{4}}\\ &=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{\sqrt{4}}\\ &=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{{2}}\\ &\approx 0.866025. \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (d)

    Find the length of \(CD\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(\frac{3\sqrt{3}}{2}\text{m}\approx 2.5981\)m
