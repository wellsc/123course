Worksheet Exercise 4. Angle Sum Identities for Sine and Cosine.

For this exercise, we will use Figure 50. This figure begins with the unit circle with center \(O=(0,0)\) and the \(x\)- and \(y\)-axes, with point \(B\) at angle \(\theta\) and point \(A\) at angle \(\theta+\phi\) on the unit circle. We then draw the following constructions.
  • Draw segment \(\overline{OA}\text{;}\)
    🔗
  • draw segment \(\overline{OB}\text{;}\)
    🔗
  • draw a perpendicular line from \(A\) to \(\overline{OB}\text{,}\) intersecting at \(C\text{;}\)
    🔗
  • draw a vertical line from \(A\) to the \(x\)-axis, intersecting at \(F\text{;}\)
    🔗
  • draw a vertical line from \(C\) to the \(x\)-axis, intersecting at \(D\text{;}\)
    🔗
  • draw a horizontal segment from \(C\) to \(\overline{AF}\text{,}\) intersecting at \(E\text{;}\)
    🔗
  • draw a vertical line from \(B\) to the \(x\)-axis, intersecting at \(G\text{.}\)
    🔗
We have highlighted \(\triangle{}AEC\) in blue and \(\triangle{}ODC\) in green.
🔗

(a)

Explain why \(\angle{}ACE\cong\angle{}OCD\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗

(b)

Explain why \(\angle{}EAC\cong\angle{}DOC\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗

(c)

Explain why \(AC=\sin(\phi)\) and \(OC=\cos(\phi)\text{.}\)
🔗
Hint.
Rotate the unit circle and all points so that \(B\) lies on the \(x\)-axis.
🔗
🔗
🔗

(d)

Explain why \(\frac{DC}{OC}=\sin(\theta)\) and \(\frac{OD}{OC}=\cos(\theta)\)
🔗
🔗

(e)

Explain why \(\frac{EC}{AC}=\sin(\theta)\) and \(\frac{EA}{AC}=\cos(\theta)\)
🔗
🔗

(f)

Note that \(\sin\left(\theta+\phi\right)=FA=FE+EA\) and \(FE=DC\text{.}\) Use Task c, Task d, Task e to express \(\sin\left(\theta+\phi\right)\) in terms of \(\sin(\theta)\text{,}\) \(\cos(\theta)\text{,}\) \(\sin(\phi)\text{,}\) and \(\cos(\phi)\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗

(g)

Note that \(\cos\left(\theta+\phi\right)=OD-DF\) and \(DF=CE\text{.}\) Use Task c, Task d, Task e to express \(\cos\left(\theta+\phi\right)\) in terms of \(\sin(\theta)\text{,}\) \(\cos(\theta)\text{,}\) \(\sin(\phi)\text{,}\) and \(\cos(\phi)\text{.}\)
🔗
(Note that in this figure, \(\theta+\phi\) is in the second quadrant, so \(\cos(\theta+\phi)\lt 0\text{.}\))
🔗
🔗
The specific geometric arguments in this activity are only valid when \(\theta\gt0\text{,}\) \(\phi\gt0\text{,}\) and \(\theta+\phi\lt \pi\text{,}\) but similar arguments can be made, making note of when values of sine and cosine are negative, for other cases, and the angle sum identities are valid for all values of \(\theta\) and \(\phi\text{.}\)
