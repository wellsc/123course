Checkpoint 57.

Recall that any vector can be expressed in terms of its length and a unit vector, and that every unit vector has the form \(\begin{bmatrix}\cos(\theta)\\ \sin(\theta)\end{bmatrix}\text{.}\) So, let \(\vec{v}_{2}\) and \(\vec{v}_{2}\) be any vectors, and we can write
\begin{align*} \vec{v}_{1}&=\ell_{1}\begin{bmatrix}\cos\left(\theta_{1}\right)\\ \sin\left(\theta_{1}\right)\end{bmatrix} & \vec{v}_{2}&=\ell_{2}\begin{bmatrix}\cos\left(\theta_{2}\right)\\ \sin\left(\theta_{2}\right)\end{bmatrix}\\ &=\begin{bmatrix}\ell_{1}\cos\left(\theta_{1}\right)\\ \ell_{1}\sin\left(\theta_{1}\right)\end{bmatrix} & &=\begin{bmatrix}\ell_{2}\cos\left(\theta_{2}\right)\\ \ell_{2}\sin\left(\theta_{2}\right)\end{bmatrix} \end{align*}
where \(\ell_{1}=\Abs{\vec{v}_{1}}\) and \(\ell_{2}=\Abs{\vec{v}_{2}}\text{.}\)
🔗
Calculate \(\vec{v}_{1}\cdot{}\vec{v}_{2}\) and use algebra and trigonometric identities to verify that
\begin{equation*} \vec{v}_{1}\cdot{}\vec{v}_{2} =\Abs{\vec{v}_{1}}\Abs{\vec{v}_{2}}\cos\left(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}\right). \end{equation*}
