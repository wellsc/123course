Checkpoint 41.

Recall that any vector can be expressed in terms of its length and a unit vector, and that every unit vector has the form \(\left[\begin{array}{r}\cos(\theta)\\ \sin(\theta)\end{array}\right]\text{.}\) So, let \(\vec{v}_{2}\) and \(\vec{v}_{2}\) be any vectors, and we can write
\begin{align*} \vec{v}_{1}&=\ell_{1}\left[\begin{array}{r}\cos\left(\theta_{1}\right)\\ \sin\left(\theta_{1}\right)\end{array}\right] & \vec{v}_{2}&=\ell_{2}\left[\begin{array}{r}\cos\left(\theta_{2}\right)\\ \sin\left(\theta_{2}\right)\end{array}\right]\\ &=\left[\begin{array}{r}\ell_{1}\cos\left(\theta_{1}\right)\\ \ell_{1}\sin\left(\theta_{1}\right)\end{array}\right] & &=\left[\begin{array}{r}\ell_{2}\cos\left(\theta_{2}\right)\\ \ell_{2}\sin\left(\theta_{2}\right)\end{array}\right] \end{align*}
where \(\ell_{1}=\Abs{\vec{v}_{1}}\) and \(\ell_{2}=\Abs{\vec{v}_{2}}\text{.}\)
🔗
Calculate \(\vec{v}_{1}\cdot{}\vec{v}_{2}\) and use algebra and trigonometric identities to verify that
\begin{equation*} \vec{v}_{1}\cdot{}\vec{v}_{2} =\Abs{\vec{v}_{1}}\Abs{\vec{v}_{2}}\cos\left(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}\right). \end{equation*}
