Solution 4.5.1.a.1.

Both \(\triangle{}OPQ\) and \(\triangle{}OMN\) are right triangles with right angles \(\angle{}OQP\) and \(\angle{}ONM\text{,}\) respectively, so \(\angle{}OQP\cong\angle{}ONM\text{.}\)
🔗
Next, \(\angle{}POQ\) and \(\angle{}MON\) are the same angle, so \(\angle{}POQ\cong\angle{}MON\text{.}\)
🔗
Finally, because the sum of the measures of the three angles in a triangle is always \(\pi\) radians (or \(180^{\circ}\)), we must have \(\angle{}OMN\cong\angle{}OPQ\text{.}\)
🔗
Because \(\angle{}OQP\cong\angle{}ONM\text{,}\) \(\angle{}POQ\cong\angle{}MON\text{,}\) and \(\angle{}OMN\cong\angle{}OPQ\text{,}\) we have \(\triangle{}OPQ\sim\triangle{}OMN\text{.}\)
